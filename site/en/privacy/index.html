Privacy policy

Applies to: the Android app “Pleiad” (package name dev.pleiad.android), the iOS app “Pleiad” (bundle ID dev.pleiad.app), and Pleiad Plus as used from them (including the parts you use by signing in to Pleiad Plus from Pleiad on your PC)
Effective: October 6, 2026
Updated: November 12, 2026

The Pleiad apps (Android and iOS; “the app”) let you use Pleiad running on your own PC (“the host”) from your phone, through a relay server (“the relay”). You can choose between a relay you set up yourself and the official relay of Pleiad Plus (“Plus”), which is run by Oz Engine Inc. (“the developer”). This policy explains what information the app handles and where it goes.

1. Information the developer receives

The app has no advertising, usage analytics or crash reporting. The developer does not use your information for tracking (following you across other companies’ apps and websites). What the developer receives depends on which relay you use.

When you use a relay you set up yourself (not using Plus)

The developer does not receive any information about you. The developer runs no server in this case, and no account sign-up is needed.

When you optionally sign in to Plus

Signing in to Plus is optional. The rest of the app works without it. You sign in with a Google or Apple account, and that company checks your password, so the developer does not receive it. When you sign in and use Plus, the developer receives the following.

  • Account information: your email address, an identifier that tells accounts apart (user ID), how you signed in (Google or Apple), and the account’s status (for example, whether it can use Plus).
  • Information for connecting: the host and device identifiers, the device’s public key, connection tokens and tickets, the IP address you connect from, when you connect, and how much data is sent. The app type and version.
  • Information about a host you register with Plus: its name, operating system type and Pleiad version (see “8. Using Plus from Pleiad on your PC”).

The developer uses this only for the app’s functions: to identify you, to check that you can use the official Plus relay, and to connect your host and device. It is not used for advertising or usage analytics.

The developer does not receive the conversations, approvals, settings, files or notification content exchanged between your host and your device. These are end-to-end encrypted between your device and your host, so the developer cannot read them even when they pass through the relay. The developer does not receive the private keys of your device or host either.

2. Where the app sends information

The app sends information off your device to the following places.

Your host (your own PC)

The app sends what you do on the host’s screen (conversations, approvals, settings and so on), files you choose to send, your device’s name (on Android, the name in your device settings, or the manufacturer and model; on iOS, the device name) and the app version. These are end-to-end encrypted between your device and your host, and only the host can read them. The keys for pairing through your own relay come only from the QR code (or code) shown on the host’s screen.

The relay

A relay you set up yourself is a server run by you (or the owner of the host), not by the developer. The official Plus relay is run by the developer. Neither relay can read the content, but to connect the two sides they can see the host and device identifiers, connection tokens, IP addresses, when and how long you connect, and how much data is sent. The connection is encrypted with TLS (https/wss).

Plus servers (only when you sign in)

When you sign in to Plus, the app sends the information listed in “1. Information the developer receives” to the developer’s servers (api.pleiad.dev, account.pleiad.dev and relay.pleiad.dev). The app uses these servers to confirm your sign-in, to get your account status and the list of your registered hosts, and to get tickets for connecting to the official relay.

Sign-in providers (only when you sign in)

Sign-in happens on a screen outside the app (Custom Tabs on Android, ASWebAuthenticationSession on iOS), using Google or Apple sign-in. What you enter there is handled by that company under its own policy (Google Privacy Policy, Apple Privacy Policy). Google’s Firebase Authentication is used to verify and renew your sign-in.

Reading QR codes

On Android, to read a pairing QR code, the app uses the QR code scanner of Google Play services (ML Kit). Images are processed on your device. Google collects device and app information, performance metrics and similar data to diagnose and measure this feature (ML Kit data disclosure, Google Privacy Policy). On iOS, the camera image is read on your device and is not sent anywhere. On both, you can pair by pasting the code instead of scanning it (hosts on Plus can be opened from the list without a QR code).

Other websites

Links on the host’s screen open in another app, such as your browser. When something shown on the host’s screen (an image in a conversation, a view an agent made, and so on) loads images or scripts from another website, your device connects to that site directly, and the site sees connection information such as your IP address. Those sites handle data under their own policies.

3. Information stored on your device

The app stores, in its own storage, the list of paired hosts (host name, relay URL and identifiers), the keys and tokens used to talk to them, and the host you opened last. On Android it also stores your notification settings. While you are signed in to Plus, it also stores the token used to keep you signed in (the short-lived token used to verify your sign-in is kept only in memory and is not stored).

On Android, keys and tokens are encrypted with a key in the Android Keystore and are not backed up. On iOS, keys and tokens are stored in the Keychain as “this device only”, so they are not carried over by backups or to another device (on a new device you pair and sign in again). On both, this is deleted when you uninstall the app. Removing a host from the list deletes that host’s information. Signing out of Plus deletes the sign-in information on your device.

4. Notifications

Notifications are used on Android only. The iOS app does not use notifications.

While notifications are on, the Android app keeps running with one ongoing notification so that it stays connected to the relay. The host encrypts each notification (its kind, the host name, the conversation name and the time) with a key for your device before handing it to the relay, so the relay cannot read it. Notifications never contain message text. You can turn notifications off at any time on the app’s Notifications screen.

5. Permissions

  • Camera (Android and iOS): used only to scan the pairing QR code.
  • Notifications (Android): used to tell you about approvals, failures and finished work. The app asks when you turn notifications on.
  • Foreground service and start at boot (Android): used, while notifications are on, to stay connected to the relay and to reconnect after a restart or an app update.
  • Microphone (Android): declared for the host’s voice call mode, but not used, because the app does not currently offer voice calls on the screen it shows through the relay.
  • Internet (Android): used to connect to the relay and the Plus servers.

6. How data is protected

Traffic between your device and your host is end-to-end encrypted with keys exchanged at pairing (the Noise protocol), and traffic to the relay and the Plus servers is encrypted with TLS. Signing in to Plus uses a method that keeps no secret in the app (PKCE) and happens on a screen outside the app. The device’s keys and tokens are stored protected by a key in the Android Keystore (Android) or in the Keychain (iOS).

When you pair through your own relay, the device and the host show the same confirmation code, and the owner of the host compares them before approving. With Plus, a device and a host signed in to the same account trust each other through Plus’s account ledger, which the developer manages, so no confirmation code is used. This means the ledger could technically be used to swap in a different key for a host. If you want to avoid this possibility, use your own relay, which pairs with a QR code and a confirmation code.

7. Retention and deletion

When you use only your own relay, the developer keeps no information about you. Information on your device is deleted when you remove a host from the list or uninstall the app. The relay does not store conversation content; while your device is offline it only holds encrypted notifications in memory for a short time (up to 15 minutes). The connection logs of your own relay (identifiers, times, amounts and so on) and the host’s conversation history are kept by the users who run them, and they can delete them.

When you sign in to Plus, the developer keeps the information listed in “1. Information the developer receives” while the account exists. You can delete your account on the account page that opens from “Manage account” in the app. Once the account is deleted, it can no longer use the official Plus relay. The sign-in information on your device is deleted when you sign out of Plus.

8. Using Plus from Pleiad on your PC

You can also sign in to Plus from Pleiad on your PC. The information the developer receives when you sign in is the same as in “1. Information the developer receives”. In addition, depending on which Plus features Pleiad on your PC uses, the following is sent to the developer’s servers.

  • Host registration: to connect to the official Plus relay, the PC’s identifier and public key, the PC’s name, its operating system type (Windows, macOS or Linux), the Pleiad version and the number of devices connected through Plus are registered against your account.
  • Sync of settings, skills and instruction files: sync keeps settings, skills and global instruction files the same across the PCs of one account. The contents are encrypted on your PC before they are uploaded and are opened on the PC that receives them. The keys are not given to the developer, so the developer cannot read the contents. The developer receives only the ciphertext, wrapped keys, item identifiers, sizes, times and the identifier of the PC that wrote them. “Secrets and variables” are not synced.
  • Automatic delegation choice and voice calls: if you use these through Plus instead of your own key, your request text and audio pass through the developer’s servers and are sent on to the company that provides the model. These are not covered by end-to-end encryption. By choosing this feature, you agree to this being sent. If you choose your own key, they do not pass through the developer’s servers.

9. AI services used by the host

The host uses AI services that you signed in to with your own accounts (such as Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity) from your PC. Instructions and conversations you send from the app may be passed to those services through the host. Those services handle data under their own policies.

10. Children

The app is not directed to children and does not knowingly collect information from children.

11. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, this page is updated and the date of the change is added below the effective date.
