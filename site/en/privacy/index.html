Privacy policy

Applies to: the Android app “Pleiad” (package name dev.pleiad.android) and the iOS app “Pleiad” (bundle ID dev.pleiad.app)
Effective: October 6, 2026
Updated: November 12, 2026

The Pleiad Android and iOS apps (“the app”) let you use Pleiad running on your own PC (“the host”) from your phone, through a relay server (“the relay”). You can choose either a relay you set up yourself or the official relay of Pleiad Plus (“Plus”), which is run by the developer. This policy explains what information the app handles and where it goes.

1. Information the developer receives

When you use your own relay, the developer does not receive any information about the app’s users. The developer runs no server for this and no account sign-up is needed. The app has no advertising, usage analytics or crash reporting.

If you optionally sign in to Plus, the servers run by the developer receive the following.

  • Your email address, user ID and how you signed in (Google or Apple)
  • Information for connecting: the host and device identifiers, your IP address, when you connected and how much data was sent

The developer uses this only for the app’s functions (signing in and connecting through the relay), and not for advertising, analytics or tracking. Conversations are end-to-end encrypted between your device and your host, so the developer cannot read them.

2. Where the app sends information

The app sends information off your device only to the following five places.

Your host (your own PC)

The app sends what you do on the host’s screen (conversations, approvals, settings and so on), files you choose to send, your device’s name (on Android, the name in your device settings, or the manufacturer and model; on iOS, the device name) and the app version. These are end-to-end encrypted between your device and your host, and only the host can read them. The keys for pairing come only from the QR code (or code) shown on the host’s screen.

The relay you set up

A relay you set up yourself is a server run by you (or the owner of the host), not by the developer. The official Plus relay is run by the developer. The relay cannot read the content, but to connect the two sides it can see the host and device identifiers, connection tokens, IP addresses, when and how long you connect, and how much data is sent. The connection is encrypted with TLS (https/wss).

Plus servers (only when you sign in)

When you sign in to Plus, the app sends the information listed in “1.” to the developer’s servers. You sign in with a Google or Apple account, using Firebase Authentication.

Google’s QR code scanner (Android)

On Android, to read a pairing QR code, the app uses the QR code scanner of Google Play services (ML Kit). Images are processed on your device. Google collects device and app information, performance metrics and similar data to diagnose and measure this feature (ML Kit data disclosure, Google Privacy Policy). You can also pair by pasting the code instead of scanning it. On iOS, the QR code is read on your device and is not sent anywhere.

Other websites

Links on the host’s screen open in another app, such as your browser. When something shown on the host’s screen (an image in a conversation, a view an agent made, and so on) loads images or scripts from another website, your device connects to that site directly, and the site sees connection information such as your IP address. Those sites handle data under their own policies.

3. Information stored on your device

The app stores, in its own storage, the list of paired hosts (host name, relay URL and identifiers), the keys and tokens used to talk to them, your notification settings, and the host you opened last. Keys and tokens are encrypted with a key in the Android Keystore on Android, and kept in the Keychain on iOS. None of this is backed up, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app. Removing a host from the list deletes that host’s information. While you are signed in to Plus, your sign-in information is also stored, and it is deleted when you sign out.

4. Notifications

Notifications are used on Android only; the iOS app does not use notifications. While notifications are on, the app keeps running with one ongoing notification so that it stays connected to the relay. The host encrypts each notification (its kind, the host name, the conversation name and the time) with a key for your device before handing it to the relay, so the relay cannot read it. Notifications never contain message text. You can turn notifications off at any time on the app’s Notifications screen.

5. Permissions

  • Camera (Android and iOS): used only to scan the pairing QR code.
  • Notifications (Android): used to tell you about approvals, failures and finished work. The app asks when you turn notifications on.
  • Foreground service and start at boot (Android): used, while notifications are on, to stay connected to the relay and to reconnect after a restart or an app update.
  • Internet (Android): used to connect to the relay.

6. How data is protected

Traffic between your device and your host is end-to-end encrypted with keys exchanged at pairing (the Noise protocol), and traffic to the relay is encrypted with TLS. When you pair through your own relay, the device and the host show the same confirmation code, and the owner of the host compares them before approving. The device’s keys and tokens are stored encrypted with a key in the Android Keystore (Android) or in the Keychain (iOS) and are not included in backups.

7. Retention and deletion

When you use your own relay, the developer keeps no information about users. When you sign in to Plus, the developer keeps the information listed in “1.” while the account exists. You can delete the account on the page that opens from “Manage account” in the app. Information on your device is deleted when you remove a host from the list or uninstall the app. The relay does not store conversation content; while your device is offline it only holds encrypted notifications in memory for a short time (up to 15 minutes). Your own relay’s connection logs (identifiers, times, amounts and so on) and the host’s conversation history are kept by the users who run them, and they can delete them.

8. AI services used by the host

The host uses AI services that you signed in to with your own accounts (such as Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity) from your PC. Instructions and conversations you send from the app may be passed to those services through the host. Those services handle data under their own policies.

9. Children

The app is not directed to children and does not knowingly collect information from children.

10. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, this page is updated and the date of the change is added below the effective date.

11. Contact

Contact us through GitHub Issues or the developer contact on the app’s Google Play or App Store listing.
